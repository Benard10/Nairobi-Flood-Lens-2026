Section 4 · How to read the map

Nearness to water is not the whole story

Red buildings overlap land marked for closer review. Near rivers, we look for nearby low ground with a higher flood-concern score. In South C, we check higher-concern land within the area that drains towards the selected outlet.

Orange buildings were checked but do not overlap that land. Even a small overlap is enough to mark a building red. This does not mean the whole building will flood.

Orange does not mean safe. These checks do not show confirmed damage, water depth or how much water a drain can carry.

How to use the map
  1. Drag to move around. Scroll or use the + and − buttons to zoom.
  2. Choose a study area from the menu, or click its point or name on the map. The building counts and selected layers update for that area.
  3. Study points open two tabs: Analysis for the findings, and History & checks for past reports and places to inspect.
  4. Click a coloured building to read its result. Click a red warning triangle for a past road-flood report.
  5. Use the layer switches to choose what appears. Read the colour scales below; distance-band colours show river distance, not flood severity.
  6. Change the background map for streets or satellite imagery. Your selected area and layer switches stay in place.
  7. The line and polygon tools at the top right measure distance and area. Finish a line with Enter; close an area by clicking its first corner.

Why some buildings are red

Red — needs closer review. Part of the mapped building overlaps land marked for review.

Orange — checked, not flagged. The building does not overlap that land. This does not mean it is safe.

Near rivers: we check river distance, height above nearby drainage and the flood-concern score. Distance alone does not make a building red.

South C: we check higher-concern land in its drainage area. There is no dedicated river mapped for this study location.
